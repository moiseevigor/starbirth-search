# Episodic accretion

> **Concept hub.** This note exists as a shared anchor — multiple paper summaries link here via their `key_concepts` frontmatter, so reading this gives the cross-paper view of one idea. Keep it short and authoritative. Long discussions go in paper notes, not here.

## Definition

Episodic accretion is the picture in which a protostar gains its mass in short episodes of high accretion rate ([[accretion-outburst]]s) separated by long quiescent phases of low rate, rather than at a constant rate. In the disk-fragmentation burst mode summarised by [[dunham-2014]] (Vorobyov & Basu 2010a), bursts last ≲100–200 yr at ṁ ≳ a few ×10⁻⁵ M⊙ yr⁻¹, and quiescent phases last 10³–10⁴ yr at ṁ ≲ 10⁻⁶ M⊙ yr⁻¹.

## Why it matters

It is the leading resolution of the [[luminosity-problem]]: most protostars are observed in quiescence, so they look under-luminous. It predicts a spread of luminosity at fixed evolutionary stage, a population of VeLLOs in quiescent phases, and burst rates that can be measured directly.

## Cross-paper view

- **Indirect evidence:** too many low-L_int sources for constant accretion ([[dunham-2008]]); an L_bol spread of 2–3 dex at fixed T_bol ([[evans-2009]]); L1451-mm would need Ṁ_acc ~300× below infall ([[pineda-2011]]); inclination and extinction corrections alone do not remove the problem ([[furlan-2016]]).
- **Direct evidence:** HOPS 383, the first Class 0 outburst ([[safron-2015]]); bursts every ~10³ yr in protostars ([[fischer-2019]], [[park-2021]]), ~440 yr in Class 0 ([[zakri-2022]]), 1.75 kyr in Class I ([[contreras-pena-2024]]), ~112 kyr in Class II ([[contreras-pena-2019]]). See [[outburst-recurrence-time]].
- **Mass in bursts:** ~25% (Offner & McKee 2011, in [[dunham-2014]]); 23–38% for HOPS 223-like bursts ([[fischer-2019]]); 3–39% to 7–100% ([[zakri-2022]]); year-scale sub-mm events add only "a few percent" ([[lee-2021]]).
- **Stage and mass dependence:** variability fraction falls with evolution in low-mass YSOs ([[park-2021]]); massive clumps peak at the YSO stage ([[lu-2024]]); high-amplitude near-IR eruptions come mostly from Class I/flat progenitors ([[lucas-2024]]).
- [[kristensen-dunham-2018]] link stochastic dwell times to non-steady-state lifetimes.

## Open questions

- [[lee-2021]] notes "a vast unexplored time domain" between year-scale sub-mm variability and ~10³ yr extreme bursts. Is there a continuous distribution of burst amplitudes and durations?
- Do all protostars take part in bursts, or only a subpopulation ([[contreras-pena-2024]] §6.3)?
