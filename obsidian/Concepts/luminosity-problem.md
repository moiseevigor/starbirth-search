# Luminosity problem

> **Concept hub.** This note exists as a shared anchor — multiple paper summaries link here via their `key_concepts` frontmatter, so reading this gives the cross-paper view of one idea. Keep it short and authoritative. Long discussions go in paper notes, not here.

## Definition

Protostars are observed to be much fainter than the [[accretion-luminosity]] expected if they built their final mass at a constant rate over the embedded lifetime. [[dunham-2014]] (§3.1): observed protostars are "generally ∼10 times less luminous than expected". The problem was first posed by Kenyon et al. (1990).

## Why it matters

It is the main indirect argument that accretion is not steady: either the rate declines with time or mass, or most mass arrives in short bursts ([[episodic-accretion]]). It therefore links luminosity functions and lifetimes to outburst statistics.

## Cross-paper view

- **Scale:** standard Shu accretion (2×10⁻⁶ M⊙ yr⁻¹ onto 0.08 M⊙, 3 R⊙) gives 1.6 L⊙; 59% of c2d Class 0/I sources with mm emission are fainter ([[evans-2009]]). With 10⁻⁵ M⊙ yr⁻¹ onto 0.25 M⊙, 3 R⊙, L_acc ~ 25 L⊙ ([[dunham-2014]]).
- **Theory:** the homogeneous 1-D collapse ends at ~10 L⊙, still too bright for Class I ([[masunaga-inutsuka-2000]]).
- **Not an observational bias:** model L_tot exceeds L_bol by ~2.5×, but "an increase in the luminosity by a factor of 2.5-3 would not solve" the problem ([[furlan-2016]]).
- **Extreme cases:** VeLLOs >10× below the standard rate ([[dunham-2008]]); a hidden YSO in L1451-mm would accrete ~300× below infall ([[pineda-2011]]).
- **Proposed solutions** ([[dunham-2014]]): slow or mass-dependent accretion, episodic accretion, inefficient accretion shocks (f_acc < 1). Tapered turbulent-core, competitive-accretion and GI-driven episodic models reproduce the luminosity function; the constant-rate isothermal sphere does not.
- **Tests via bursts:** HOPS 383 ([[safron-2015]]); burst rates and burst mass fractions ([[fischer-2019]], [[park-2021]], [[zakri-2022]], [[contreras-pena-2024]]).

## Open questions

- What fraction of the missing luminosity is resolved by bursts versus by a declining mean rate? Burst mass-fraction estimates range from a few percent ([[lee-2021]], year-scale events) to >50% ([[fischer-2019]], HOPS 383-like bursts).
