# The Hi-GAL compact source catalogue I: physical properties of inner-Galaxy clumps

## Abstract

Hi-GAL is a large-scale survey of the Galactic plane, performed with Herschel in five infrared continuum bands between 70 and 500 µm. We present a band-merged catalogue of spatially matched sources and their properties derived from fits to the spectral energy distributions (SEDs) and heliocentric distances, based on the photometric catalogs presented in Molinari et al. (2016a), covering the portion of Galactic plane −71.0° < ℓ < 67.0°. The band-merged catalogue contains 100922 sources with a regular SED, 24584 of which show a 70 µm counterpart and are thus considered proto-stellar, while the remainder are considered starless. Thanks to this huge number of sources, we are able to carry out a preliminary analysis of early stages of star formation, identifying the conditions that characterise different evolutionary phases on a statistically significant basis. We calculate surface densities to investigate the gravitational stability of clumps and their potential to form massive stars. We also explore evolutionary status metrics such as the dust temperature, luminosity and bolometric temperature, finding that these are higher in proto-stellar sources compared to pre-stellar ones. The surface density of sources follows an increasing trend as they evolve from pre-stellar to proto-stellar, but then it is found to decrease again in the majority of the most evolved clumps. Finally, we study the physical parameters of sources with respect to Galactic longitude and association with spiral arms, finding only minor or no differences between the average evolutionary status of sources in the fourth and first Galactic quadrants, or between "on-arm" and "inter-arm" positions.

## TL;DR

This paper turns the [[molinari-2016]] single-band lists into a physical catalogue of 100,922 inner-Galaxy clumps. Each clump has a grey-body temperature, and where a distance exists, a mass and luminosity. It also defines the Hi-GAL evolutionary rule: a 70 µm detection means protostellar, otherwise starless; a starless clump above the Larson mass–radius threshold is pre-stellar. Appendix C quantifies the two distance biases that make the rule unreliable beyond ~5 kpc. It is the primary reference for the rule and its caveats.

## Problem

How do you get physical properties and a first evolutionary classification for ~10⁵ unresolved far-IR clumps across the inner plane? The only inputs are continuum photometry and CO-based kinematic distances. And how badly does distance distort the classification (§1, §3.5, App. C)?

## Approach

- **Band merging (§3.1).** Starting from 500 µm, the closest shorter-wavelength counterpart falling inside the longer-wavelength source ellipse is assigned, step by step down to 70 µm. An SED is kept only if it has ≥ 3 consecutive Herschel detections among 160–500 µm, a "regular" shape, and lies in the common PACS+SPIRE area. Fluxes at 350/500 µm are rescaled to the 250 µm source size.
- **Reliability split (§3.1).** "High reliability": 62,438 sources. "Low reliability": 38,484 sources with only three Hi-GAL fluxes. This includes the "SPIRE-only" sources, detected at 250/350/500 µm only. All low-reliability sources are starless by construction.
- **Ancillary photometry (§3.3).** 21/22/24 µm fluxes (MSX, WISE, MIPSGAL, summed within the 250 µm ellipse) are used only for luminosity. ATLASGAL 870 µm and BGPS 1.1 mm fluxes are used in the fit.
- **Targeted deeper extraction (§3.5).** At 160 µm this recovered 9,705 detections and 9,992 upper limits. At 70 µm, towards 160 µm-detected sources lacking 70 µm: 912 more detections and 76,215 upper limits.
- **Distances (§3.4).** Russeil et al. (2011) scheme with the Brand & Blitz (1993) rotation curve. The V_LSR is that of the brightest ¹²CO/¹³CO component (GRS and Exeter-FCRAO for ℓ < 55° and ℓ > 55°; NANTEN in the 4th quadrant, 4′ pixels). The near/far ambiguity is solved with known-distance sources or extinction maps; otherwise far is chosen by default and flagged "bad quality".
- **SED fit (§4).** Single-temperature grey body with β = 2 fitted to λ ≥ 160 µm. κ_ref = 0.1 cm² g⁻¹ at 300 µm, gas-to-dust ratio 100 included. Fit ranges 5 K ≤ T ≤ 40 K and 5 µm ≤ λ₀ ≤ 350 µm, where λ₀ is the wavelength at which the optical depth equals 1. If λ₀ < 50.6 µm the optically thin form is used. Luminosity for protostellar sources is the grey-body integral for λ ≥ 160 µm plus the observed 21–160 µm SED. Sources without distance get a placeholder d = 1 kpc for distance-independent quantities.

## Key result

- **Counts (Abstract, §9 Summary, Table 1).** 100,922 regular SEDs: 24,584 protostellar (70 µm counterpart) and 76,338 starless. The Summary splits the starless into 16,667 bound (pre-stellar) and 59,671 unbound. Table 1 totals: protostellar 15,834 with distance and 8,750 without.
- **Classification rule (§3.5).** An object with a 70 µm counterpart is labelled proto-stellar with high confidence (citing Dunham et al. 2008, Ragan et al. 2013, Svoboda et al. 2016). The paper says the rule works for nearby objects but breaks down beyond roughly 4–5 kpc. Bound (pre-stellar) if M(r) > 460 M⊙ (r/pc)^1.9, Larson's third relation.
- **Distances (§3.4).** 57,065 of 100,922 sources (56%) have a distance, but for 35,904 of these the near/far ambiguity is unresolved and the far distance is assigned by default. There are no distances for −10.2° < ℓ < 14.0°.
- **70 µm sensitivity caveat (§3.5 fn 4, App. C1, Fig. C2).** A grey body with 50 M⊙, 15 K and β = 2 at 10 kpc gives 0.02 Jy at 70 µm but 0.84 Jy at 250 µm, so it is seen by SPIRE but not PACS. The adopted typical PACS 70 µm sensitivity limit is 0.2 Jy. With that limit, grey bodies with M ≤ 50 M⊙ and T ≤ 16 K are undetectable at d ≳ 5 kpc. Only e.g. M ≥ 500 M⊙ with T ≥ 16 K, or M ≥ 50 M⊙ with T > 19 K, remain detectable to 13 kpc. Conclusion: a minor but significant fraction of distant low-mass or cold "starless" clumps are misclassified protostellar clumps.
- **Confusion caveat (App. C1, Fig. C1).** Sources at d < 4 kpc were virtually moved outwards, and counted as merged when separated by less than 18″ (the 250 µm beam). The protostellar number fraction then rises with a fitted slope of 0.005 kpc⁻¹. This is proposed as a correction for global quantities such as the SFR.
- **Mass completeness (App. C2, Eq. C2).** M_compl(T, d)/M⊙ = 0.0705 (F_compl,350/Jy)(d/kpc)² [exp(41.1094/(T/K)) − 1]. It is evaluated for the extreme 350 µm 90% flux limits of 13.08 and 0.65 Jy from [[molinari-2016]].
- **Evolutionary indicators (§6.2, §6.5).** Median grey-body T is 11.7 K (pre-stellar) vs 15.1 K (protostellar); means are 12.0 vs 16.0 K. 99% of pre-stellar sources are below 18.1 K. "MIR-dark" protostellar sources (70 µm detected, no 21/22/24 µm counterpart) are 10% of protostellar sources, with median T = 14.8 K. H II-region candidates are protostellar sources with L_bol/M ≥ (L/M)_P ≡ 22.4 L⊙/M⊙, the peak of the CORNISH-counterpart distribution. L_bol/L_smm < 100: 99.96% of pre-stellar sources and 86% of protostellar sources. So Herschel-only SEDs bias the classification towards "Class 0-like".
- **Galactic trends (§8, Summary xi–xiii).** Fourth vs first quadrant and on-arm vs inter-arm differences are small. A low pre-/protostellar number ratio in longitude bins with median Σ > 0.2 g cm⁻² is read as a possible sign that high-density clumps spend only a short time in the pre-stellar stage.

## Assumptions

- A 70 µm detection implies internal star formation; its absence implies starless (§3.5).
- A single-temperature grey body with β = 2 and a fixed κ₃₀₀ describes the cold dust. Alternative literature opacities change masses by factors from 6 to 0.6 (§4).
- Kinematic distances from the brightest CO component along the line of sight, with far distance by default when unresolved (§3.4).
- Larson (1981) M–r relation as the boundedness criterion. There is no virial or pressure analysis.

## Limitations / scope

- The classification is photometric only, and the authors call it too clear-cut. Contamination between classes is unavoidable, and it inflates the overlap of the property distributions (§3.5).
- The opposing distance biases (70 µm incompleteness vs blending) are illustrated but not corrected in the catalogue (App. C).
- Distances: 44% of sources have none, and most of those assigned are far-by-default. Superseded by the Mège et al. (2021) distances in [[elia-2021]].
- Very bright or saturated sources and irregular SEDs are dropped. The catalogue under-represents the most evolved clumps (§3.2, §6.5).
- Physical sizes: most sources are clumps (0.2–3 pc), not cores. They host protoclusters, not single protostars (§6.1).
- I read the full text of §1–§6.2, §6.5–§7 intro, §8.1 intro, the Summary and Appendix C. The §6.3–§6.4 (mass function, surface density) and §7.1–§7.4 details were skimmed.

## Replication evidence

Partial. [[elia-2021]] re-derived the same quantities with the same method and new distances, and recovered the same qualitative pre- vs protostellar segregation (medians of T and L/M). There is no independent-team replication of the 70 µm rule at Hi-GAL scale in this set. The lower-limit nature of starless labels is echoed by spectroscopic infall/outflow detections in 70 µm-quiet clumps, cited in [[elia-2021]].

## Why this paper matters

This paper defines [[hi-gal-evolutionary-classification]], the most widely used Galaxy-wide prestellar/protostellar split for clumps. It also states both failure modes. First, the 70 µm sensitivity floor of ~0.2 Jy hides low-mass or cold protostellar clumps beyond ~5 kpc, which is exactly the regime of low-luminosity embedded protostars. Second, beam blending at large distance turns starless+protostellar pairs into one "protostellar" clump. Any lifetime estimated from the Hi-GAL number ratio ([[statistical-lifetime]]) inherits both biases, with opposite signs.

## Open questions surfaced

- What is the net sign and size of the combined bias (70 µm incompleteness vs blending) on the Galaxy-wide protostellar fraction, as a function of distance? Only the blending half gets a slope (0.005 kpc⁻¹).
- What fraction of 70 µm-dark "pre-stellar" clumps at d > 5 kpc host protostars detectable with deeper 70 µm data or spectroscopic infall/outflow tracers?
- Can a Herschel-only criterion (L_bol/M ≥ 22.4 L⊙/M⊙) identify H II regions reliably outside the CORNISH footprint?
