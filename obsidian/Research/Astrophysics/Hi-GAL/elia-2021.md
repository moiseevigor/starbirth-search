# The Hi-GAL compact source catalogue II: the 360° clump catalogue

## Abstract

We present the 360° catalogue of physical properties of Hi-GAL compact sources, detected between 70 and 500 𝜇m. This release not only completes the analogous catalogue previously produced by the Hi-GAL collaboration for −71° ≲ ℓ ≲ 67°, but also meaningfully improves it thanks to a new set of heliocentric distances, 120808 in total. About a third of the 150223 entries are located in the newly added portion of the Galactic plane. A first classification based on detection at 70 𝜇m as a signature of ongoing star-forming activity distinguishes between protostellar sources (23 per cent of the total) and starless sources, with the latter further classified as gravitationally bound (pre-stellar) or unbound. The integral of the spectral energy distribution, including ancillary photometry from 𝜆 = 21 to 1100 𝜇m, gives the source luminosity and other bolometric quantities, while a modified black body fitted to data for 𝜆 ≥ 160 𝜇m yields mass and temperature. All tabulated clump properties are then derived using photometry and heliocentric distance, where possible. Statistics of these quantities are discussed with respect to both source Galactic location and evolutionary stage. No strong differences in the distributions of evolutionary indicators are found between the inner and outer Galaxy. However, masses and densities in the inner Galaxy are on average significantly larger, resulting in a higher number of clumps that are candidates to host massive star formation. Median behaviour of distance-independent parameters tracing source evolutionary status is examined as a function of the Galactocentric radius, showing no clear evidence of correlation with spiral arm positions.

## TL;DR

This is the whole-plane (360°) Hi-GAL clump catalogue: 150,223 sources, of which 120,808 (80%) have distances from Mège et al. (2021). It uses the same 70 µm protostellar/starless rule and Larson boundedness cut as [[elia-2017]]. It states the 70 µm sensitivity caveat explicitly: undetected 70 µm emission below F₇₀ ≈ 0.2 Jy, which misses low-luminosity Class 0 objects. It also turns pre-/protostellar number ratios into clump lifetimes (about 60%/40% of the time pre-/protostellar). Use it as the current census and for its lifetime and distance-bias discussion.

## Problem

Complete the Hi-GAL physical catalogue to the whole plane, including the outer Galaxy. Replace the [[elia-2017]] distances with the improved Mège et al. (2021) set. Then compare inner- and outer-Galaxy clump populations using distance-dependent quantities (mass, size, luminosity) and distance-independent ones (T, L_bol/M, L_bol/L_smm, T_bol, Σ) (§1).

## Approach

- **SED selection (§2.1).** Same pipeline as [[elia-2017]]. Only the common PACS+SPIRE area, whose boundaries were slightly enlarged. Positional band merging. SEDs need ≥ 3 adjacent fluxes in 160–500 µm, a concave-down shape and F₃₅₀ − F₅₀₀ > 0. MIR counterparts come from MSX, WISE and MIPSGAL, and sub-mm ones from ATLASGAL and BGPS. MSX/WISE replace MIPSGAL in the outer Galaxy.
- **Fit (§2.1).** Modified black body (MBB) with β = 2, fitted to λ ≥ 160 µm. Optically thick form if λ₀ < 50.6 µm, as in [[elia-2017]]. Fits pinned at the grid edges (T = 5 or 40 K) go to the low-reliability list, as do all three-flux SEDs.
- **Distances (§2.1, §3.1).** Mège et al. (2021) assign a V_LSR per source from all available spectroscopic surveys plus a morphological analysis. Parallax or maser distances are used where known. Otherwise a kinematic distance with the Russeil et al. (2017) rotation curve. The near/far ambiguity is resolved with H I self-absorption or distance–extinction data. Only line detections at 3σ are used. Distances are rejected for R_GC > 20 kpc, and for tangent-point assignments whose V_LSR is more than 10 km s⁻¹ from the tangent velocity.
- **Inner/outer split (§2.2).** Inside or outside the Solar circle, R_GC = 8.34 kpc. Sources without distance are assigned by the majority class in their longitude bin.
- **Classification (§2.1).** Protostellar if detected at 70 µm, otherwise starless. Starless clumps are pre-stellar if M(r) > 460 M⊙ (r/pc)^1.9 (Larson 1981), otherwise unbound.

## Key result

- **Counts (Abstract, Tables 1–2).** 150,223 sources: 94,604 high-reliability and 55,619 low-reliability.
  - High-reliability: 35,186 protostellar, 49,289 pre-stellar, 10,129 unbound.
  - Low-reliability: 226 protostellar, 31,265 pre-stellar, 24,128 unbound.
  - Protostellar sources are 23 per cent of the total (35,412 of 150,223).
- **Distances (§3.1).** 120,808 of 150,223 have a distance (80%), vs 57,065 of 100,922 (57%) in [[elia-2017]]. Of these, 88,131 are in the inner Galaxy and 32,677 in the outer Galaxy. The inner-Galaxy distance histogram is bimodal because of the near/far ambiguity. [[elia-2022]] quotes the Mège et al. median distance errors as 0.54 kpc absolute and 16% relative.
- **70 µm caveat (§2.1).** The classification can be affected by confusion and by lack of 70 µm sensitivity for F₇₀ ≲ 0.2 Jy. Low-mass Class 0 objects have low luminosities (Dunham et al. 2013) and may stay undetected at 70 µm. Infall and outflow signatures have been found in 70 µm-quiet clumps. Hence starless sources are really *candidate* starless clumps.
- **Distance trend of the ratio (§3.1, Fig. 5).** The pre-stellar/protostellar ratio decreases with distance, which the authors read as blending dominating over 70 µm incompleteness. The protostellar share is 47% in the outer Galaxy vs 40% in the inner Galaxy.
- **Sizes (§3.2).** 80.1% of sources are clumps, 19.7% cores and 0.3% clouds, using the Bergin & Tafalla (2007) definitions.
- **Masses (§3.3).** At equal distance, the median outer-Galaxy mass is smaller by a factor of 1.1 to 4.3. Mass–radius thresholds for massive star formation: the Krumholz & McKee (2008) (KM08) cut is met by 4% of the catalogue. The Kauffmann & Pillai (2010) (KP10) cut is met by 62% of protostellar and 56% of pre-stellar sources with distances; these fall to 55% and 47% with OH5 opacities. 57 sources pass the Bressert et al. (2012) proto-cluster cut, 18 of them in the outer Galaxy.
- **Lifetimes (§3.4, Fig. 14).** The H II-region lifetime relation log(t_HII/yr) = −0.13 log(L/L⊙) + 6.1 (Mottram et al. 2011), evaluated at L_bol/M = 22.4 L⊙/M⊙, is split in proportion to the pre-/protostellar populations per mass bin.
  - A pre-/protostellar ratio of ~1.5 up to ~10³ M⊙ gives 60% of the time pre-stellar and 40% protostellar, consistent with Battersby et al. (2017).
  - A ratio of ~1.2 at 10⁴ M⊙ gives 55%/45%.
  - The crossing mass of the two lifetime curves depends on distance: ~2 × 10³ M⊙ for 2–5 kpc and ~140 M⊙ for 8–11 kpc.
- **Distance-independent medians (§4, Table 4).**
  - L_bol/M: pre-stellar 0.2 vs protostellar 2.6 L⊙/M⊙ in the inner Galaxy; 0.1 vs 3.1 in the outer Galaxy.
  - MBB T: 11.4 vs 15.2 K (inner); 10.5 vs 15.3 K (outer).
  - H II-region candidates (L_bol/M ≥ 22.4): 2,806 inner and 869 outer. 2,560 of them match the Anderson et al. (2014) WISE H II catalogue, 2,192 of those with radio emission.
- **Radial trends (§5, Summary vi–viii).** The star-formation fraction, defined as the ratio of protostellar clumps to all clumps, is not monotonic in R_GC. It dips near 7 kpc and rises beyond the Solar circle. 967 sources are in the far outer Galaxy, R_GC ≥ 13.5 kpc (677 after excluding |ℓ| < 10° and |ℓ − 180°| < 10°).

## Assumptions

- A 70 µm detection is taken as an indicator of star formation. Boundedness uses only the Larson relation, with no pressure or magnetic terms (§2.1).
- Constant gas-to-dust ratio of 100 and κ₃₀₀ = 0.1 cm² g⁻¹. The authors note that a gas-to-dust ratio rising with R_GC would change the outer-Galaxy densities (Summary ii).
- The mapping from population ratio to time assumes a steady state and an H II-region clock (Mottram et al. 2011) evaluated at a single L/M value (§3.4).

## Limitations / scope

- The classification is photometric only. The 70 µm floor (~0.2 Jy) and blending at large d push the labels in opposite directions, and neither is corrected in the catalogue.
- Distance-dependent quantities carry kinematic-distance errors. Mass scales as d², so a factor-2 change in distance empties or overfills the proto-cluster region (§3.3). No global clump mass function is attempted.
- The lifetime numbers depend on the chosen absolute clock. Relative fractions are robust to the clock but not to classification bias.
- I read the full §1–§4.2 and the Summary. §4.3–§5 details (L_bol/L_smm, T_bol, Σ, radial profiles) were skimmed via Table 4 and the Summary.

## Replication evidence

Partial. The inner-Galaxy results are consistent with [[elia-2017]] under new distances (same method, same team). The pre-/protostellar time split (~60/40) agrees with [[battersby-2017]] (BGPS-based). The decreasing ratio with clump mass is qualitatively the same as [[svoboda-2016]], but with a shallower slope.

## Why this paper matters

It is the definitive Galaxy-wide Hi-GAL clump census and the direct input to the Hi-GAL Milky Way SFR ([[elia-2022]]). For the star-birth question it matters in two ways. First, it quantifies how the [[hi-gal-evolutionary-classification]] and the resulting [[statistical-lifetime]] estimates depend on distance: the lifetime crossing mass moves by more than an order of magnitude between 2–5 and 8–11 kpc. Second, it explicitly acknowledges that 70 µm-quiet "starless" clumps can host low-luminosity [[class-0-protostar]]s. So Hi-GAL starless counts are upper limits on truly starless clumps.

## Open questions surfaced

- How large is the population of 70 µm-dark clumps hosting protostars below F₇₀ ≈ 0.2 Jy, as a function of distance and clump mass?
- Distance-binned lifetimes disagree strongly (crossing mass 140 M⊙ vs 2 × 10³ M⊙). Which is closest to the truth once blending is modelled?
- Why does the star-formation fraction dip near R_GC ≈ 7 kpc and rise in the outer Galaxy? Is it physical, a gas-to-dust effect, or a distance or selection artefact?
- A 57-source massive proto-cluster candidate list is deferred to future work.
