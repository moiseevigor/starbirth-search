# The Star Formation Rate of the Milky Way as seen by Herschel

## Abstract

We present a new derivation of the Milky Way's current star formation rate (SFR) based on the data of the Hi-GAL Galactic plane survey. We estimate the distribution of the SFR across the Galactic plane from the star-forming clumps identified in the Hi-GAL survey and calculate the total SFR from the sum of their contributions. The estimate of the global SFR amounts to 2.0 ± 0.7 M⊙ yr−1, of which 1.7 ± 0.6 M⊙ yr−1 coming from clumps with reliable heliocentric distance assignment. This value is in general agreement with estimates found in the literature of last decades. The profile of SFR density averaged in Galactocentric rings is found to be qualitatively similar to others previously computed, with a peak corresponding to the Central Molecular Zone and another one around Galactocentric radius Rgal ∼ 5 kpc, followed by an exponential decrease as log(ΣSFR /[M⊙ yr−1 kpc−2]) = a Rgal /[kpc] + b, with a = −0.28 ± 0.01. In this regard, the fraction of SFR produced within and outside the Solar circle is 84% and 16%, respectively; the fraction corresponding to the far outer Galaxy (Rgal > 13.5 kpc) is only 1%. We also find that, for Rgal > 3 kpc, our data follow a power law as a function of density, similarly to the Kennicutt-Schmidt relation. Finally, we compare the distribution of the SFR density across the face-on Galactic plane and those of median parameters, such as temperature, luminosity/mass ratio and bolometric temperature, describing the evolutionary stage of Hi-GAL clumps. We found no clear correlation between the SFR and the clump evolutionary stage.

## TL;DR

A Milky Way SFR built by summing per-clump contributions over all 70 µm-detected ("protostellar") Hi-GAL clumps from [[elia-2021]]. Result: 1.7 ± 0.6 M⊙ yr⁻¹ from clumps with distances, and 2.0 ± 0.7 M⊙ yr⁻¹ including an estimate for 5,532 clumps without distances. This agrees with the literature compilations [[chomiuk-povich-2011]] (1.9 ± 0.4) and [[licquia-newman-2015]] (1.65 ± 0.19). It also gives an operational per-clump formula, SFR_cl ∝ M_cl^0.74.

## Problem

Estimate the current Galactic SFR directly and self-consistently from a whole-plane far-IR census of star-forming clumps, rather than by renormalising heterogeneous literature values. Then map how it is distributed in ℓ, b and R_gal (§1).

## Approach

- **Input (§2.1).** All 29,880 protostellar clumps in [[elia-2021]] with heliocentric distance, plus 5,532 protostellar clumps without one. Protostellar means detected at 70 µm. Distances are from Mège et al. (2021), with median absolute error 0.54 kpc and relative error 16%.
- **Per-clump SFR (§2.2).** Method of Veneziani et al. (2013, 2017). Each clump follows a theoretical evolutionary track in the L_bol–M diagram (Molinari et al. 2008), with a Monte Carlo treatment of protocluster multiplicity. The SFR contribution is final stellar mass divided by elapsed time. The authors distil it into Eq. 1: SFR_cl = (5.6 ± 1.4) × 10⁻⁷ (M_cl/M⊙)^(0.74±0.03) M⊙ yr⁻¹.
- **Distance bias check (§2.2).** Baldeschi et al. (2017a) moved Gould Belt regions to 0.75–7 kpc. Clump-based SFRs from the displaced maps stayed within a factor 2 of YSO-count SFRs.
- **Clumps without distance (§2.2.1).** 100 realisations each assign simulated distances drawn from the known-distance distribution. Extreme cases put all such clumps at one common distance, 1–13 kpc.
- **Incompleteness (App. A).** The first/fourth-quadrant SFR beyond d = 12 kpc is compared with the symmetric region on the far side of the Galaxy.

## Key result

- **Global SFR (§2.2, §2.2.1, Abstract).**
  - Clumps with distance: 1.7 ± 0.6 M⊙ yr⁻¹. The error comes from propagating the clump-mass errors and the Eq. 1 parameter errors.
  - Of this, inner Galaxy (R_gal < 8.34 kpc): 1.5 M⊙ yr⁻¹ (84%); outer Galaxy: 0.3 M⊙ yr⁻¹ (16%).
  - Added term for the 5,532 clumps without distance: mean 0.22 M⊙ yr⁻¹ (range 0.20–0.23, s.d. 0.01 over 100 realisations; the per-realisation error is 0.08).
  - Final: **2.0 ± 0.7 M⊙ yr⁻¹**.
  - Bounds on the added term: 0 if all such clumps sit at 1 kpc, 0.57 M⊙ yr⁻¹ if all at 13 kpc. It is 0.13 or 0.19 at the median or mean known distance, and 0.17 ± 0.06 if the |ℓ| < 20° ones sit at the Galactic Centre.
- **Incompleteness correction (App. A).** At T = 16 K, d = 10 kpc and a 5 Jy flux limit, the mass completeness is ~90 M⊙. The far-side comparison gives a missing SFR of ~0.15 M⊙ yr⁻¹, an 8% correction. Applying the same 8% to 2.0 gives 2.13 M⊙ yr⁻¹.
- **Stated lower-limit caveats (§2.2.1).** (i) Distance-dependent mass completeness. (ii) Protostellar clumps undetected at 70 µm are misclassified as starless and excluded from the sum. (iii) Clumps without distance, handled above.
- **Literature comparison (Table 1, §2.3).** Consistent within errors with every Chomiuk & Povich (2011) normalised value except Smith et al. (1978). Also consistent with 1.9 ± 0.4 ([[chomiuk-povich-2011]]), 1.65 ± 0.19 ([[licquia-newman-2015]]) and 2.1 ± 0.4 (Noriega-Crespo 2013). The authors' summary: the current Milky Way SFR lies in 1.5–2.5 M⊙ yr⁻¹.
- **CMZ (§3.1, Table 2).** For |ℓ| < 1°, |b| < 0.5°: 0.06 ± 0.02 M⊙ yr⁻¹, vs 0.09–0.10 from Barnes et al. (2017). For Longmore et al. (2013)'s boxes: 0.11 and 0.12 vs their 0.035 and 0.06. About 90% of the SFR comes from the inner 120° of longitude.
- **Radial profile (§3.2, Fig. 6).** The Σ_SFR maximum is at the CMZ, then a dip at 1–4 kpc, then a peak near 5 kpc. For R_gal ≥ 5 kpc: log Σ_SFR = a R_gal + b, with a = −0.28 ± 0.01 and b = −0.30 ± 0.16. That slope is an exponential scale length of 1.55 kpc. The cumulative SFR reaches 50%, 90% and 99% at R_gal = 5.8, 9.2 and 13.4 kpc. The far outer Galaxy (R_gal > 13.5 kpc) contributes 1%.
- **Kennicutt–Schmidt (§3.3, Fig. 7).** Σ_SFR vs Σ_H2 in 1-kpc rings for R_gal > 3 kpc (93% of the SFR) has slope n = 1.14 ± 0.07. There is no clear relation with H I or with total gas.
- **Evolutionary stage (§3.4, Summary).** There is no significant correlation between local SFR and the median clump evolutionary indicators. The local SFR follows the available clump mass.

## Assumptions

- Only 70 µm-detected clumps form stars now. The SFR is the sum of per-clump final-mass / track-time contributions from the Molinari et al. (2008) / Veneziani et al. (2017) tracks (§2.2).
- The clump masses of [[elia-2021]] use a constant gas-to-dust ratio of 100 and κ₃₀₀ = 0.1 cm² g⁻¹.
- Clumps without a distance follow the known-distance distribution (§2.2.1). Approximate circular symmetry is assumed for the incompleteness correction (App. A).
- The Kennicutt–Schmidt test uses X_CO = 10²⁰ cm⁻² (K km s⁻¹)⁻¹, constant with radius, for Σ_H2 (Miville-Deschênes et al. 2017).

## Limitations / scope

- By the authors' own statement it is a lower limit. 70 µm-dark protostellar clumps and clumps below the mass-completeness limit (~90 M⊙ at 10 kpc, 16 K) are missing.
- The ±0.6–0.7 error covers mass and Eq. 1 parameter errors only. Systematics from the tracks, IMF, dust opacity and gas-to-dust ratio are not in the error bar. The distance-bias validation is at the factor-2 level.
- No SFR is placed in the two bar-dominated lobes where the rotation curve forbids the observed velocities, which contributes to the 1–4 kpc dip.
- I read the full text of §1–§3.3, the Summary and Appendix A. §3.4 (face-on maps vs evolutionary indicators) was skimmed.

## Replication evidence

Partial. Numerically consistent with the extrapolation of Noriega-Crespo (2013) from two Hi-GAL tiles (2.1 ± 0.4), and with independent tracers compiled in [[chomiuk-povich-2011]] and [[licquia-newman-2015]]. It is the only whole-plane clump-based estimate; no independent team has repeated it.

## Why this paper matters

This is the Hi-GAL answer to "how many stars form per year in the Galaxy". It is bottom-up and independent of the ionising-photon calibrations that dominate earlier values, yet lands at 2.0 ± 0.7 M⊙ yr⁻¹. It also shows that the Galactic SFR is concentrated: 84% inside the Solar circle, half within R_gal ≈ 5.8 kpc. For any star-birth rate argument, it sets the expected rate of new protostars, with the caveat that 70 µm-dark star formation is excluded by construction.

## Open questions surfaced

- How much SFR hides in 70 µm-dark but star-forming clumps? That is the sign-opposite counterpart of the blending bias discussed in [[elia-2017]].
- Is the steep exponential scale length (1.55 kpc vs ~2.15 kpc for the stellar disk in [[licquia-newman-2015]]) physical, or caused by the constant gas-to-dust ratio and X_CO?
- The per-clump prescription SFR_cl ∝ M_cl^0.74 has not been tested against resolved YSO counts beyond ~7 kpc.
