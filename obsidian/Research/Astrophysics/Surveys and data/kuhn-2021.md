# SPICY: The Spitzer/IRAC Candidate YSO Catalog for the Inner Galactic Midplane

## Abstract

We present ∼120,000 Spitzer/IRAC candidate young stellar objects (YSOs) based on surveys of the Galactic midplane between ℓ∼255° and 110°, including the GLIMPSE I, II, and 3D, Vela-Carina, Cygnus X, and SMOG surveys (613 square degrees), augmented by near-infrared catalogs. We employed a classification scheme that uses the flexibility of a tailored statistical learning method and curated YSO datasets to take full advantage of IRAC's spatial resolution and sensitivity in the mid-infrared ∼3–9 μm range. Multi-wavelength color/magnitude distributions provide intuition about how the classifier separates YSOs from other red IRAC sources and validate that the sample is consistent with expectations for disk/envelope-bearing pre–main-sequence stars. We also identify areas of IRAC color space associated with objects with strong silicate absorption or polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbon emission. Spatial distributions and variability properties help corroborate the youthful nature of our sample. Most of the candidates are in regions with mid-IR nebulosity, associated with star-forming clouds, but others appear distributed in the field. Using Gaia DR2 distance estimates, we find groups of YSO candidates associated with the Local Arm, the Sagittarius-Carina Arm, and the Scutum-Centaurus Arm. Candidate YSOs visible to the Zwicky Transient Facility tend to exhibit higher variability amplitudes than randomly selected field stars of the same magnitude, with many high-amplitude variables having light-curve morphologies characteristic of YSOs. Given that no current or planned instruments will significantly exceed IRAC's spatial resolution while possessing its wide-area mapping capabilities, Spitzer-based catalogs such as ours will remain the main resources for mid-infrared YSOs in the Galactic midplane for the near future.

## TL;DR

117,446 IR-excess YSO candidates (SPICY) over 613 deg² of the inner Galactic midplane, selected by random forests trained on MYStIX YSOs vs field contaminants using JHK (2MASS/UKIDSS/VVV) + cryogenic IRAC colours. Effective depth [4.5] ≲ 14.5 mag (candidates span [4.5] = 7.3–13.7, 1–99%). Class split by α(4.5–24): 15,943 Class I, 23,810 flat, 59,949 Class II, 5,352 Class III, 12,392 uncertain. Purity is unmeasured; in SMOG it recovers only 32% of Winston+ candidates while 97% of its own are shared; up to ~35% could be misclassified if Robitaille+2008 AGB labels are right.

## Problem

YSOs in the inner Galactic midplane are largely uncatalogued because of high extinction, crowding/confusion, and multiple clouds along the line of sight. Previous GLIMPSE-wide searches (Robitaille et al. 2008, [4.5]−[8.0] ≥ 1, 8 μm > 10 mJy) were bright-limited and contaminated by AGB stars. Can a uniform, spatially-unbiased YSO list be built from IRAC photometry to several kpc?

## Approach

- **Data** (§II.1): cryogenic IRAC 3.6/4.5/5.8/8.0 μm GLIMPSE "Catalog" (reliable, not "Archive") photometry — GLIMPSE I (31,184,509 sources), II (19,067,533), 3D (20,403,915), Vela-Carina (2,001,032), Cygnus X (3,913,559), SMOG (2,512,099). PSF FWHM 1.66–1.98″ vs "∼6″" for WISE. Deeper Cygnus X / SMOG sources trimmed to GLIMPSE limits (bright: 7, 6.5, 4.0, 4.0 mag; 3σ detection limits 15.5, 15.0, 13.0, 13.0 mag in the four bands). Adopted faint limit [4.5] = 14.5 mag.
- **NIR** (§II.2): 2MASS, UKIDSS GPS, VVV (VIRAC DR1), 1″ match; saturation cuts; selection run separately for IRAC+2MASS / +UKIDSS / +VVV and merged.
- **IR-excess pre-filter** (§III.1): ≥ 4 of 7 bands incl. 3.6 & 4.5; colour-excess rules (e.g. [3.6]−[4.5] − 0.408 > error, where 0.408 is the reddening of this colour for A_V ≈ 30 mag); then reject sources fit by reddened Castelli & Kurucz photospheres (keep χ² per point > 4). Leaves 319,251 (2MASS+IRAC), 188,701 (UKIDSS+IRAC), 257,334 (VVV+IRAC) inputs.
- **Training** (§III.2): YSO labels from MYStIX (Carina, NGC 6611, M17, NGC 6530, M20, NGC 6357, NGC 6334, RCW 38, RCW 36, DR 21; 0.7–2.7 kpc); non-YSO labels from MYStIX-field non-members and star-formation-free control fields. 2MASS+IRAC: 2,865 YSOs, 3,436 MYStIX-field objects, 7,718 other field objects.
- **Missing data** (§III.3): copula imputation (sbgcop); only 57%/46%/29% of 2MASS/UKIDSS/VVV training rows are complete.
- **Classifier** (§III.4–5): random forest (1500 trees, caret), covariates J−H, H−Ks, Ks−[3.6], [3.6]−[4.5], [4.5]−[5.8], [5.8]−[8.0], [3.6]. *YSO score* = fraction of trees voting YSO; candidate if score > 0.5 in any of the three forests. 80/20 train/test validation: all classifiers AUC > 0.9 (AUC = area under the receiver-operating-characteristic curve, true-positive vs false-positive rate; 0.5 = random).
- **Validation** (§V–IX): colour–magnitude diagrams, Gaia DR2 CMD, 24 μm colours, spectral index α = d log(λF_λ)/d log λ from [4.5]–[24] (α ≈ 0.55([4.5]−[24]) − 2.94) or [4.5]−W4 or [4.5]−[8.0]; environment clustering of 3′×3′ cutouts; HDBSCAN groups; ZTF variability; comparison with other catalogs.

## Key result

Catalog (§IV, §V.5):
- "there are 117,446 candidate YSOs and 180,997 probable contaminants" among IR-excess sources (§IV); ~90,000 are new identifications (§X).
- Magnitude range of candidates: "[3.6]=8.3–14.9 mag, [4.5]=7.3–13.7 mag, [5.6]=6.4–12.9 mag, and [8.0]=5.5–12.2 mag (1%–99% quantiles)" (§II.1).
- Class counts (using Greene et al. α boundaries): 15,943 Class I, 23,810 flat-spectrum, 59,949 Class II, 5,352 Class III, 12,392 uncertain class (§V.5).
- ~2000 candidates with [5.8]−[8.0] < 0 flagged as possible strong silicate absorbers (§V.6); a PAH-coloured "finger" at 1.5 ≲ [5.8]−[8.0] ≲ 2.6 identified (§V.2, §V.7).

Completeness / contamination indicators (§II.3, §V, §IX):
- Longer-wavelength counterparts: "Only 30% of our IRAC candidates have counterparts in the 24 μm MIPS band, and 8% have reliable 22 μm WISE photometry" — WISE photometry is "strongly affected by crowding and nebulosity" in the plane; Koenig & Leisawitz quality cuts leave holes around YSO clusters (§II.3).
- vs WISE all-sky YSOs (Marton et al. 2016): within the SPICY footprint Marton finds ~75,000 Class I–II WISE sources vs ~110,000 IRAC Class I–II here; only ~5000 in common at 2″ (§IX).
- vs Robitaille et al. (2008): their 10 mJy 8 μm limit would discount 73% of SPICY YSOs; SPICY finds > 4× more candidates in GLIMPSE I/II. Of 16,670 R08 red sources, 13,290 (80%) re-selected: 8,637 (92%) of R08 "YSO" and 4,653 (64%) of R08 "AGB". "Assuming the Robitaille et al. [150] classifications are accurate, and extrapolating to fainter objects, this could suggest that up to ∼35% of our YSO candidates are misclassified" — but 33% of the disputed 4,653 lie in spatial YSO clusters.
- vs SMOG (Winston et al.): "97% of our YSO candidates were also classified as YSOs by Winston et al. [186], while only 32% of their YSO candidates were classified as YSOs by us" (1524 vs 4648 objects); SPICY misses bluer and fainter objects, peaking at [4.5] = 13 vs 14.5 mag (training-set depth limitation).
- Faint sources are preferentially classified as contaminants, possibly inheriting a bias from the MYStIX training set "which only includes YSOs out to ∼3 kpc" (§V).
- Colour cuts truncate the blue end, so the catalog is "not … sensitive to certain classes of YSOs, including some YSOs with anemic disks or pre–main-sequence stars without disks" (§V.2).
- Gaia DR2: ~36,000 candidates (~30%) detected; 7686 with ϖ/σ_ϖ > 3 lie mostly above the 1 Myr isochrone (§V.3).

Environment (§VI.1): cutout clustering → ~57% cloud-like, ~28% nebulosity-free, ~13% mixed, ~2% outliers; nebulosity-free sources have preferentially more negative α (KS p < 0.01).

Variability (§VIII): 7,585 candidates with ZTF DR3 r-band light curves; 88% above the field-star median σ_var; 1695 high, 914 moderate, 4976 low/insignificant variability (thresholds 3× and 2× field median); 76% of high-variability stars have positive skewness (dipper-like). Example outburst SPICY 116663 with > 3 mag amplitude.

Structure (§VII, §X): ~400 HDBSCAN groups; distances from Gaia DR2 mean astrometry; groups at the Local (Orion), Sagittarius-Carina and Scutum-Centaurus arms; most candidates at ≳ 1 kpc.

Flags (Appendix A): GLIMPSE "close source flag" and per-band detection counts kept in Table 1 but not used to filter; sources with < 2 detections in a band are less reliable.

## Assumptions

- MYStIX IR-excess members (0.7–2.7 kpc massive complexes) are representative of midplane YSOs out to several kpc.
- Non-YSO labels (MYStIX non-members without X-ray emission; control fields) are clean.
- Imputed colours are nuisance parameters; the authors "do not advocate for the use of these colors in other contexts" (§III.3).
- No spatial clustering used in selection (deliberately, to avoid biasing clustering studies).

## Limitations / scope

- Contamination (AGB stars, PAH knots, shocked-H₂ knots, galaxies) is not measured; "formal assessment of contamination requires followup observations" (§IV). Extragalactic contamination argued to be low because GLIMPSE is shallow and extinction is high (Appendix B.2).
- Completeness vs deeper Spitzer data is low (32% recovery of SMOG/Winston candidates) — the catalog is conservative (purity-leaning) and depth-limited.
- Deeply embedded (Class 0-like) protostars without detections at 3.6/4.5 μm are excluded by construction (≥ 4 bands incl. 3.6 and 4.5 required).
- Cryogenic-mission IRAC only (2004–2009 epoch); GLIMPSE 360 warm-mission data excluded; outer Galaxy covered by SFOG instead.
- Read in full via arXiv HTML (v2 text); figures via captions.

## Replication evidence

Partial. [[contreras-pena-2024]] builds its VVV Class I sample from SPICY, Robitaille et al. (2008) and Marton et al. (2016) and notes that Kuhn et al. "also expect a low level of contamination of extragalactic sources", i.e. relies on the authors' own contamination argument rather than an independent purity measurement. [[lucas-2024]] also cites SPICY. No spectroscopic purity estimate is known in this vault.

## Why this paper matters

SPICY is the largest uniform mid-IR YSO list for the inner Galactic plane and is widely used as a membership prior or veto list for time-domain searches (VVV, NEOWISE, Gaia alerts). Its explicit comparisons quantify two things any user must carry forward: (1) WISE is badly hampered in the plane (only 8% of SPICY sources have reliable W4; low overlap with WISE YSO lists), and (2) SPICY trades completeness for purity — it recovers ~1/3 of candidates from deeper Spitzer data and requires 3.6 + 4.5 μm detections, so very red, faint, or deeply embedded sources are under-represented.

## Open questions surfaced

- Spectroscopic purity of SPICY as a function of [4.5] magnitude, environment class, and α (the ~66,000 candidates with G < 19 are WEAVE/4MOST-accessible; §X).
- Completeness for the most embedded protostars (Class 0-like) that lack 3.6 μm detections — how many midplane protostars fall below SPICY's selection by construction?
- Whether the ~35% misclassification upper bound (from R08 AGB labels) holds at fainter magnitudes where R08 has no labels.
- Does mid-IR (NEOWISE/unTimely) variability discriminate YSOs from AGB contaminants better than ZTF optical variability, given only ~30% of candidates are optically visible?
