# The JCMT Transient Survey: Four Year Summary of Monitoring the Submillimeter Variability of Protostars

## Abstract

We present the four-year survey results of monthly submillimeter monitoring of eight nearby (< 500 pc) star-forming regions by the JCMT Transient Survey. We apply the Lomb-Scargle Periodogram technique to search for and characterize variability on 295 submillimeter peaks brighter than 0.14 Jy beam⁻¹, including 22 disk sources (Class II), 83 protostars (Class 0/I), and 190 starless sources. We uncover 18 secular variables, all of them protostars. No single-epoch burst or drop events and no inherently stochastic sources are observed. We classify the secular variables by their timescales into three groups: Periodic, Curved, and Linear. For the Curved and Periodic cases, the detectable fractional amplitude, with respect to mean peak brightness, is ∼4 % for sources brighter than ∼ 0.5 Jy beam⁻¹. Limiting our sample to only these bright sources, the observed variable fraction is 37 % (16 out of 43). Considering source evolution, we find a similar fraction of bright variables for both Class 0 and Class I. Using an empirically motivated conversion from submillimeter variability to variation in mass accretion rate, six sources (7 % of our full sample) are predicted to have years-long accretion events during which the excess mass accreted reaches more than 40 % above the total quiescently accreted mass: two previously known eruptive Class I sources, V1647 Ori and EC 53 (V371 Ser), and four Class 0 sources, HOPS 356, HOPS 373, HOPS 383, and West 40. Considering the full protostellar ensemble, the importance of episodic accretion on few years timescale is negligible, only a few percent of the assembled mass. However, given that this accretion is dominated by events of order the observing time-window, it remains uncertain as to whether the importance of episodic events will continue to rise with decades-long monitoring.

## TL;DR

Four years (Dec 2015 – Jan 2020) of roughly monthly SCUBA-2 850 µm monitoring of 8 Gould Belt regions yields 18 secular variables among 295 bright peaks, **all protostars**. There are **zero** variables among 190 starless peaks or 22 disks, and no single-epoch bursts. For bright sources (> 0.5 Jy/beam), 37% of protostars vary, with Class 0 (38%) and Class I (30%) statistically indistinguishable. About 7% of protostars have year-scale accretion events adding > 40% to quiescent mass. The implied year-scale bursts recur every ~50 yr per source, but they contribute only a few percent of total assembled mass.

## Problem

How common is year-scale accretion variability in deeply embedded protostars? Sub-mm continuum traces the envelope's response to luminosity, so it probes sources too extincted for optical or near-IR work. Does variability depend on evolutionary stage (starless, Class 0, Class I, Class II), and how much mass does it assemble (§1, §5.6)?

## Approach

- **Survey (§2).** SCUBA-2 at 850 µm has a 14.6″ beam. It monitored IC 348, NGC 1333, NGC 2024, NGC 2068, OMC 2/3, Ophiuchus, Serpens Main and Serpens South, monthly (Serpens Main twice monthly). Each field is a ~30′ Pong map with ~14 mJy/beam noise. Post-processing aligns maps to < 1″ and cuts relative flux calibration to **2%**. The 450 µm data are deferred.
- **Sample (§2).** From 1665 FellWalker peaks, 295 have mean peak brightness > 0.14 Jy/beam. These are classified by matching within 10″ to SED-classified YSOs (Dunham et al. 2010; Stutz et al. 2013; Megeath et al. 2016).
- **Secular variability (§3.1).** A Lomb-Scargle periodogram is used, with the false-alarm probability (FAP) threshold 0.1% for sinusoid and linear fits. Variables are grouped as Periodic, Curved or Linear.
- **Stochastic variability (§3.2).** Single-epoch outliers above 5σ_fid are counted, together with the overall dispersion χ²_80%.
- **Completeness (§4, App. A).** The minimum detectable amplitude is derived as a function of brightness, period and number of epochs.
- **Evolutionary stage (§5.2).** The proxy is extinction-corrected T_bol, taken from Furlan et al. 2016 (Orion) and Mowat 2018 (others).
- **Accretion conversion (§5.6).** The mass ratio is M_burst/M_quiescent = 0.5([1 + A_burst]⁴ − 1). The exponent of ~4 comes from Contreras Peña et al. 2020.

## Key result

- **Starless and disk null (§3.1, Fig. 3).** Starless cores and disks all sit in the region with "no robust evidence for secular variability" (FAPs > 0.1%). All 18 secular variables are protostars.
- **No stochastic events (§3.2).** Six candidate > 5σ single-epoch events were all rejected or reassigned. Two were image-reconstruction artefacts, and these included "the only non-protostellar potential variable source in our sample". Three were focus/beam-pattern artefacts within 30″ of sources brighter than 5 Jy/beam. One (HOPS 373) was part of a longer secular brightening.
- **Detectability (Abstract, §4).** The detectable fractional amplitude is "∼4 %" for sources > 0.5 Jy/beam. Completeness is flat above 0.5 Jy/beam. For short-period sinusoids, the detectable amplitude is 1.30 σ_fid.
- **Variable fraction (Abstract, §5.6).** At least 20% of protostars show secular variation (18/83). The fraction is 37% (16/43) for protostars > 0.5 Jy/beam.
- **Stage dependence (§5.2).** Among sources > 0.5 Jy/beam, the counts are 0/2 Class II, 3/10 (30%) Class I and 13/34 (38%) Class 0 secular variables. The difference between Class 0 and Class I "is insignificant (p = 0.61)".
- **Regional dependence (§5.1).** Among bright protostars, only OMC 2/3 has a statistically low variable fraction.
- **Comparison with NEOWISE (§5.5).** Of 39 protostars in common, 23 (59%) are MIR variables over 6.5 yr, compared with 55% over all protostars in [[park-2021]]. Only 8/39 (20%) are sub-mm variables. Most MIR variability is irregular, whereas all sub-mm variables are secular. The authors attribute this to NEOWISE's ~6-month cadence and to envelope thermal smoothing. The fractional sub-mm change is lower by a factor of ~5.5 than in the MIR.
- **Mass accretion (§5.6).** The smallest-amplitude variables add ~10% of the quiescent mass. The six strongest add > 40%: V1647 Ori 125% per ~3 yr burst, EC 53 60% per ~0.75 yr burst, HOPS 356 85%, HOPS 373 70%, West 40 45%, HOPS 383 40%. Roughly 7% (6/83) of protostars show order-unity mass-accretion variability on year timescales. The authors "predict that for a given source, years-long bursts occur on average about every ∼ 50 yrs", accounting for "only a few percent of the accreted mass".
- **Contrast with MIR extreme bursts (§5.6).** The authors contrast this with Scholz et al. 2013 and [[fischer-2019]], where "less than 1% of the protostars were seen to vary on half a decade timescales", implying ~1000 yr between extreme events. They note "a vast unexplored time domain" between the two regimes.

## Assumptions

- Sub-mm peak brightness tracks envelope temperature, which scales as a power of protostellar luminosity. Luminosity is taken as a roughly linear proxy for accretion rate (§5.6).
- For periodic fits, quiescence is set at the light-curve minimum (A_burst = 2A) and bursts last half a period. For linear fits, the burst spans ~2 yr with A_burst = 4 × slope (§5.6).
- Peaks within 10″ of an SED-classified YSO inherit its class. Other peaks are "starless" (§2).

## Limitations / scope

- **Beam and confusion.** The 14.6″ beam at < 500 pc blends multiple protostars, e.g. IRAS 4A as a double source (Appendix notes). Bright neighbours within 30″ generate spurious residuals when focus is poor (§3.2).
- **Map-reconstruction artefacts.** Spurious large-scale features from makemap reconstruction produced false single-epoch events. These affected the only non-protostellar variable candidate (§3.2).
- **Brightness-dependent completeness.** Older protostars are fainter in the sub-mm, so raw stage comparisons are biased. Only > 0.5 Jy/beam sources are used for stage trends, leaving small n (2 Class II, 10 Class I, 34 Class 0) (§4, §5.2).
- **Window.** The 4.5 yr window truncates long-period events. Amplitudes for HOPS 356, 373 and 383 and West 40 (periods > 2× the window) are lower limits (§5.6).
- **Starless null bounds.** The null applies only to 850 µm secular or single-epoch changes in bright peaks over 4 yr. It says nothing about fainter cores or about embedded sources too faint to be classified as protostars.

## Replication evidence

Partial. The NEOWISE companion [[park-2021]] independently finds high protostellar MIR variability (55%) with the same secular/stochastic scheme. The sub-mm to MIR link is quantified in Contreras Peña et al. 2020. Later JCMT Transient Survey follow-ups were not read for this note.

## Why this paper matters

This is the reference measurement of year-scale variability in the most embedded protostars. It is the only one here at a wavelength where extinction is irrelevant, so it can see Class 0 sources that near- and mid-IR surveys cannot interpret. It establishes three results useful as priors. First, **starless cores do not vary** at the ~4% level over 4 yr: 0/190, a clean negative control. Second, sub-mm variability does not clearly increase from Class I to Class 0 among bright sources. Third, year-scale accretion variability is common (≥ 20–37%) but mass-negligible.

The paper frames the gap between frequent small (~50 yr) and rare large (~1000 yr, [[fischer-2019]]) events. It also flags four Class 0 sources (HOPS 356, 373, 383 and West 40) with eruptive-level amplitudes.

## Open questions surfaced

- Does year-scale sub-mm variability connect smoothly to the rare ~kyr FUor-class events, or are there distinct episodic timescales (§5.6)? Decade-long monitoring is needed.
- Does improving relative calibration from 2% to 1% reveal lower-amplitude variables, or variables among starless peaks (§6)?
- Are the four high-amplitude Class 0 sources (HOPS 356, 373, 383 and West 40) in FUor-type events? Longer baselines and ALMA structure are needed (§5.6).
- Why is the OMC 2/3 bright-protostar variable fraction anomalously low (§5.1)?
