# Sample and Statistical Analysis on NEOWISE Variability of ATLASGAL Sources

## Abstract

This study focuses on the analysis of mid-infrared variability in a sample of high-mass young stellar objects (YSOs) associated with the cataloged sources from the APEX Telescope Large Area Survey of the Galaxy (ATLASGAL). The Near-Earth Object Wide-field Infrared Survey Explorer Reactivation Mission (NEOWISE) database was used to explore the long-term mid-infrared variability of these high-mass YSOs at a half-year scale. After matching with NEOWISE photometric measurements, a total of 2230 ATLASGAL sources were selected for the variability analysis, out of which 717 were identified as variables. The derived proportions of variables at different evolutionary stages show that the variability rate of high-mass YSOs is highest during the YSO stage and decreases with evolution toward the H II region stage, resembling the behavior of low-mass YSOs. The variables can be classified into six types based on their light curves, divided into two categories: secular (linear, sin, sin+linear) and stochastic variables (burst, drop, and irregular). The magnitude–color variations observed in ∼160 secular variables can be mainly divided into "bluer when brighter/redder when dimming" and "redder when brighter/bluer when dimming," likely originating from changes in accretion rate or the effect of extinction due to obscuration. Moreover, several episodic accretion candidates were selected for further observational studies.

## TL;DR

The authors take the ATLASGAL clump catalogue with evolutionary stages from Urquhart et al. 2022 (quiescent, protostellar, YSO, H II). They match ~8000 clumps to NEOWISE W2 single-exposure light curves (~6-month cadence) and keep 2230 with good data. Of these, 717 (32%) are variable by the Stetson index. The variable fraction peaks at the **YSO stage (50%)** and is lower at the protostellar (27%), quiescent (18%) and H II (18%) stages. This is the first stage-resolved mid-IR variability census of massive clumps. Its most provocative number is that 18% of "quiescent" clumps host a variable mid-IR source, and the paper does not analyse contamination for this.

## Problem

Does mid-IR variability, a proxy for episodic accretion and extinction changes, depend on the evolutionary stage of high-mass star-forming clumps? Does it mirror the stage dependence seen in low-mass YSOs (e.g. [[park-2021]])?

## Approach

- **Parent catalogue (§2.1).** ATLASGAL has 5007 clumps staged by visual inspection of multiwavelength images (Urquhart et al. 2022): quiescent 1218, protostellar 1010, YSO 1543, H II region 1236.
- **NEOWISE matching (§2.2).** The initial search radius is 10″, and "this search radius may cover two to three NEOWISE sources for some ATLASGAL sources". K-means clustering of positions followed by a 3″ re-match isolates one counterpart. Sources need ≥ 100 W2 measurements over ≥ 10 epochs, with intra-epoch standard deviation < 0.15 mag. This leaves 2230 sources. NEOWISE cadence is ~6 months, with ≥ 12 exposures per epoch.
- **Variability test (§2.3).** The Stetson index is used, with threshold μ + 3σ = 8.64.
- **Classification (§3).** Secular variables are linear, sin or sin+linear, with R² ≥ 0.6 and the simpler model preferred. Stochastic variables are burst, drop or irregular. Burst means W2_median − W2_min > 0.7 ΔW2; drop means W2_max − W2_median > 0.7 ΔW2, where ΔW2 = W2_max − W2_min. This scheme is adapted from the low-mass NEOWISE work of [[park-2021]].
- **Diagnostics (§4).** The analysis compares variable properties (SD/σ and ΔW2) by stage and variables with and without 6.7 GHz methanol masers (§4.1.2). It also examines W2 against W1−W2 colour–magnitude behaviour (§4.2).

## Key result

- **Variable fraction by stage (Table 1).** "Quiescent" is ATLASGAL's no-embedded-mid-IR-source stage.

  | Stage | Variables / total | Rate |
  |---|---|---|
  | Quiescent | 45/248 | 18.14% |
  | Protostellar | 95/353 | 26.91% |
  | YSO | 444/888 | 50% |
  | H II region | 133/741 | 17.95% |
  | Total | 717/2230 | 32.15% |

- **Summary statement (§5).** The variability rate peaks at 50% in the YSO stage and falls to 18% at the H II region stage.
- **Variable types (Table 1, §3).** There are 422 secular variables (linear+ 20, linear− 47, sin 252, sin+linear 103) and 295 stochastic variables (burst 36, drop 12, irregular 247). Applying the burst criterion to all light curves adds bursts within secular types, for 104 bursts in total (§3.3).
- **Comparison with low-mass YSOs (§4.1).** Periodic (sin-type) variables are 50% of the sample, "twice" the combined curved+periodic share (25%) among low-mass YSOs. Burst variables are 14%, against 8% for low-mass YSOs.
- **Mid-IR detection rate (§4.1.1).** The mid-IR detection rate in early high-mass stages is low: ~20% for quiescent clumps, rising to ~60% for H II regions, against ~77% for low-mass YSOs. The authors attribute this to heavier envelope extinction.
- **Amplitude trend (§4.1.3, Fig. 4).** SD/σ and ΔW2 first increase and then decrease with evolution, peaking at the YSO stage. Low-mass YSOs show larger SD/σ and ΔW2 than the high-mass sample.
- **Colour behaviour (§4.2, Table 3).** Secular variables split into three groups. "Redder when brighter / bluer when dimming" (RB/BD, 84) and "bluer when brighter / redder when dimming" (BB/RD, 64) are attributed to changing accretion rate or obscuration; 42 more show different periods in the two bands (PD).
- **Masers (§4.1.2, §4.3).** "In the quiescent stage, no 6.7 GHz methanol maser source was matched." Episodic-accretion candidates are mostly associated with 6.7 GHz masers. The exemplar is G24.33+0.14, which brightened by ~1.3 mag over ~3 yr.

## Assumptions

- ATLASGAL stage labels, assigned by visual multiwavelength inspection (Urquhart et al. 2022), are correct and apply to the matched NEOWISE point source.
- The NEOWISE point source within 3″ of the clump position is the clump's embedded object.
- The Stetson threshold μ + 3σ separates astrophysical from instrumental variability for bright, possibly extended Galactic-plane sources.

## Limitations / scope

- **Starless-stage variables are not vetted.** From the extraction, the paper does not test whether the 45 variable "quiescent" sources are foreground or background stars, unrelated YSOs or mis-associations. The quiescent class is defined by the absence of embedded mid-IR emission (Urquhart), so a W2 point source ≤ 3″ from a quiescent clump is either a mis-classification or an unrelated field star. The 18% rate is therefore not evidence of variability in starless clumps. (Researcher observation.)
- **Selection bias.** Only 248/1218 quiescent and 353/1010 protostellar clumps have usable NEOWISE light curves, versus 888/1543 YSO and 741/1236 H II. Stage fractions are conditional on mid-IR detection, which is itself stage-dependent (§4.1.1).
- **Resolution and confusion.** WISE W2 resolution is ~6″, and the paper notes that 10″ can enclose 2–3 NEOWISE sources. K-means plus a 3″ re-match reduces but does not remove blending. From the extraction, there is no explicit treatment of nebulosity, PAH or extended-emission contamination or W2 saturation (Researcher observation; bright H II regions are prone to all three).
- **Cadence.** Variations shorter than 6 months are aliased. The authors note short-period variables may be classified as stochastic.
- **Full-text access.** Section-level quotes come from a summarising fetch of the IOPscience HTML, not from reading the PDF directly.

## Replication evidence

Partial. The rise-then-fall of variable fraction with evolution qualitatively matches the low-mass NEOWISE result of [[park-2021]], where protostars are more variable than disks. No independent high-mass stage-resolved census exists in this vault.

## Why this paper matters

This is the only study in this set that measures variability against clump evolutionary stage for massive star formation. The stage-by-stage variable fractions of 18/27/50/18% could serve as priors for any NEOWISE-based search in Galactic-plane clumps (compare Hi-GAL clump classifications).

The data-quality lessons matter as much as the headline. At ATLASGAL and WISE resolution, multiple W2 sources fall within a clump. The earliest stages are mostly undetected in W2 (~20%). A non-trivial fraction of "quiescent" clumps carry variable point sources that probably are not the clump's own protostar. It is the natural high-mass counterpart to the null starless result of [[lee-2021]] at 850 µm (0/190 starless peaks variable).

## Open questions surfaced

- What are the 45 variable "quiescent" sources? A positional-offset, colour and Gaia-parallax test would separate field stars from mis-classified embedded YSOs.
- Does the protostellar-stage fraction (27%) rise once blending is removed? Unblended higher-resolution mid-IR (e.g. unTimely/Spitzer cross-checks) could test this.
- Do the maser-associated burst candidates show the slow (~2 yr) rises seen in near-IR eruptions ([[lucas-2024]])?
- How do the stage fractions change if they are computed relative to all clumps rather than only W2-detected ones?
