# Quantifying Variability of YSOs in the Mid-IR Over Six Years with NEOWISE

## Abstract

Variability in young stellar objects (YSOs) can be caused by various time-dependent phenomena associated with star formation, including accretion rates, geometric changes in the circumstellar disks, stochastic hydromagnetic interactions between stellar surfaces and inner disk edges, reconnections within the stellar magnetosphere, and hot/cold spots on stellar surfaces. We uncover and characterize ∼1700 variables from a sample of ∼5400 YSOs in nearby low-mass star-forming regions using mid-IR light curves obtained from the 6.5-years NEOWISE All Sky Survey. The mid-IR variability traces a wide range of dynamical, physical, and geometrical phenomenon. We classify six types of YSO mid-IR variability based on their light curves: secular variability (Linear, Curved, Periodic) and stochastic variability (Burst, Drop, Irregular). YSOs in earlier evolutionary stages have higher fractions of variables and higher amplitudes for the variability, with the recurrence timescale of FUor-type outbursts (defined here as ∆W1 or ∆W2 > 1 mag followed by inspection of candidates) of ∼1000 years in the early embedded protostellar phase. Known eruptive young stars and subluminous objects show fractions of variables similar to the fraction (∼ 55%) found in typical protostars, suggesting that these two distinct types are not distinct in variability over the 6.5-year timescale. Along with brightness variability, we also find a diverse range of secular color variations, which can be attributed to a competitive interplay between the variable accretion luminosity of the central source and the variable extinction by material associated with the accretion process.

## TL;DR

NEOWISE W1/W2 light curves (14 six-monthly epochs, 6.5 yr) for 5398 YSOs in 20 Gould Belt regions: 1734 variables, classified into Linear / Curved / Periodic (secular) and Burst / Drop / Irregular (stochastic). Variable fraction falls with evolution — 55% protostars, 33% disks, 15% PMS+E. FUor-type outbursts (ΔW1 or ΔW2 > 1 mag, then visual/multi-survey vetting) in protostars recur every ~1000 yr (τ = 478–2295 yr depending on k and N; e.g. 683 yr, 90% 356–1579 yr for k = 6, N = 735).

## Problem

How common, how large and what kind is mid-IR variability across YSO evolutionary classes, and what does a multi-epoch all-sky survey imply for the recurrence of FUor-type outbursts in protostars? (§1)

## Approach

- **Sample (§2):** 6927 YSOs from Megeath et al. (2012, Orion), Esplin & Luhman (2019, Taurus), Dunham et al. (2015, 18 c2d/Gould Belt regions; see [[evans-2009]]); reclassified as Class 0/I incl. flat (P), Class II (D), Class III+Evolved (PMS+E; AGB contamination suspected).
- **Quality cuts:** match radius 3″; within 2σ of mean offset; positional scatter < 0.3″; > 5 W2 epochs; mean W2 uncertainty < 0.2 mag → 5398 YSOs (~13.6% P, 64.1% D, 22.3% PMS+E) (§2).
- **Cadence:** NEOWISE-R ([[mainzer-2014]]) visits every 6 months, 10–20 exposures per visit over ≲ few days; upper/lower 15% of exposures clipped, remaining 70% averaged per epoch; error = mean error ⊕ std. dev. (§2).
- **Variability metrics (§3):**
  - SD/σ = standard deviation of W2 flux over the light curve divided by mean flux uncertainty (Johnstone et al. 2018 stochasticity indicator);
  - ΔW2 = max − min magnitude; first cut ΔW2/σ(W2) > 3 (3894 pass);
  - Lomb–Scargle periodogram with Baluev false-alarm probability FAP_LSP < 10⁻²; minimum period 200 d;
  - linear least-squares fit, FAP_Lin < 10⁻⁴.
- **Classes (§4):** Linear (FAP_Lin < 10⁻⁴); Curved (FAP_LSP < 10⁻², period 1200–4800 d); Periodic (FAP_LSP < 10⁻², period < 1200 d); Burst: (median − min mag) > 0.8 ΔW2; Drop: (max − median) > 0.8 ΔW2; Irregular: remaining with SD/σ > 3. Secular trends subtracted and residuals re-tested → combined types.
- **Outburst recurrence (§6):** candidates with ΔW1 or ΔW2 > 1 mag (227: 104 P, 112 D, 11 PMS+E), visual inspection of long-term light curves incl. Spitzer, 2MASS, DENIS, UKIDSS, VHS, CMC15, APASS, Pan-STARRS, ZTF (blind to prior eruptive classification); τ from Eq. 9 and 90% CI by integrating Eq. 8 of [[contreras-pena-2019]].

## Key result

- **Variable counts (Table 2):** P: 403 variables (54.8%) — Linear 37, Curved 103, Periodic 6, Burst 13, Drop 0, Irregular 244; D: 1146 (33.1%); PMS+E: 185 (15.4%); total 1734. ~29% of variables are secular (P 36%, D 21%, PMS+E 63%) (§4.1).
- Linear protostars: 32 negative vs 5 positive slopes — "indicative of a longer decaying timescale than the bursting timescale" (§4.1.1).
- "about half of protostars (55%) are variable in contrast with ∼33% and ∼15% of disks and PMS+E sources" (§5.3); amplitude of Curved/Periodic variability largest for Class 0/I (Fig. 12).
- **Short-timescale example:** [MGM2012]77 brightened 1 mag in W2 within one ~1-day visit (≈2 h exposure cadence) (§2).
- **Eruptive vs subluminous:** 15/25 FUors/EXors (60%) and 40/73 VeLLOs/LLSs (55%) variable; FUor/EXor variables mostly secular (about half Linear(−)), VeLLO/LLS variables more than half Irregular (§5.4).
- **FUor-type candidates:** 20 by eye (4 disks rejected on Hα Ṁ / low L; 16 protostars). Counted: 2 confirmed FUors (HOPS 383, IRAS 18270−0153) + 7 with strong FUor photometric characteristics (Serpens SMM 1, 2MASS J04283510+3625065, 2MASS J21013280+6811204, SSTgbs J21470601+4739394, HOPS 41 [ΔW2 > 4 mag], HOPS 297, HOPS 20); 7 excluded (V1647 Ori, HBC 340, HOPS 343, etc.) (§6).
- **Recurrence timescale τ (Table 5; t = 6.5 yr):**

| N protostars | k | τ (yr) | 90% interval (yr) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1059 | 9 | 688 | 403–1321 |
| 1059 | 6 | 983 | 511–2270 |
| 1059 | 2 | 2295 | 751–13519 |
| 735 | 9 | 478 | 281–919 |
| 735 | 6 | 683 | 356–1579 |
| 735 | 2 | 1593 | 521–9385 |

- No FUor outbursts in 4477 Class II disks over 6.5 yr, consistent with the ~112 kyr Class II rate of [[contreras-pena-2019]] (§6).
- Agrees with [[fischer-2019]] (~1000 yr) and with ~1000 yr H₂ knot spacing (§6).

## How outbursts were separated from other variability

- Amplitude threshold > 1 mag (following Scholz et al. 2013; [[fischer-2019]]) to discard hot-spot / inner-disk variability (§6).
- Light-curve morphology: long-duration rise or post-burst decline (FUor-like) vs repetitive / short (EXor, MNor) or dips (extinction) (§5.2, §6).
- Multi-wavelength cross-check: e.g. LkHα 337 — 1 mag mid-IR rise coincident with optical **drops**, attributed to inner-disk scale-height change rather than accretion (§6, Fig. 16).
- W1−W2 colour tracks: accretion (bluer when brighter, temperature-driven) vs extinction (redder); cyclic clockwise/counter-clockwise tracks like EC 53 interpreted as disk build-up and draining (§5.5).
- Spectroscopic/physical vetoes (Hα-based Ṁ too low; L_bol ≤ 0.18 L⊙ during bright state) rejected the 4 disk candidates (§6).

## Assumptions

- Class labels from heterogeneous input catalogues; Class III contaminated by AGB stars (Appendix C).
- Poisson burst statistics with the same rate for all protostars; t = 6.5 yr for all.
- Epoch averaging over ~1 day adequately represents each 6-month visit.

## Limitations / scope

- 6-month cadence: cannot characterise variability < 6 months; periods > 1200 d not confirmable as periodic (§3.2, §5.2).
- Bright/red candidate protostars faint in W1/W2 excluded (e.g. Megeath "red candidate protostars") → biased against the most embedded Class 0.
- ~6″ WISE PSF blends close companions/nebulosity; [[zakri-2022]] reports HOPS 12 and HOPS 124 bursts were not identified here.
- FUor classification of 7 of 9 counted outbursts is photometric only.

## Replication evidence

Yes, for the headline rate: independently reproduces [[fischer-2019]]'s ~1000 yr protostellar interval in a sample ~2–3× larger across 20 regions; [[zakri-2022]] (Spitzer, Orion) finds 879 yr for all protostars. The evolutionary trend in variable fraction/amplitude agrees with YSOVAR (Wolk et al. 2018) and VVV (Contreras Peña et al. 2017).

## Why this paper matters

The first homogeneous NEOWISE census of YSO mid-IR variability across evolutionary classes, providing a reusable classification scheme (SD/σ, ΔW2, FAP thresholds, Burst/Drop criteria) and a per-source variability table. It extends the protostellar outburst-rate estimate beyond Orion and shows that eruptive (FUor/EXor) and subluminous (VeLLO) objects are not separable by variability fraction over 6.5 yr.

## Open questions surfaced

- Short-timescale (intra-visit, ~hours–days) mid-IR variability — deferred to a separate paper (§2).
- Spectroscopic confirmation of the 7 photometric FUor candidates, which moves τ by ~×3 (k = 2 vs 9).
- Full analysis of secular W1−W2 colour cycles as accretion/extinction diagnostics (§5.5).
