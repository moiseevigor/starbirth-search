# The Evolution of Protostars: Insights from Ten Years of Infrared Surveys with Spitzer and Herschel

## Abstract

(Verbatim from the arXiv v2 PDF.)

Stars form from the gravitational collapse of dense molecular cloud cores. In the protostellar phase, mass accretes from the core onto a protostar, likely through an accretion disk, and it is during this phase that the initial masses of stars and the initial conditions for planet formation are set. Over the past decade, new observational capabilities provided by the Spitzer Space Telescope and Herschel Space Observatory have enabled wide-field surveys of entire star-forming clouds with unprecedented sensitivity, resolution, and infrared wavelength coverage. We review resulting advances in the field, focusing both on the observations themselves and the constraints they place on theoretical models of star formation and protostellar evolution. We also emphasize open questions and outline new directions needed to further advance the field.

## TL;DR

The PPVI synthesis of Spitzer (c2d, Gould Belt, Orion, Taurus) and Herschel (HGBS, HOPS) protostar surveys. Headline numbers:

- **Class 0+I duration 0.42–0.54 Myr** (a half-life, anchored to a Class II lifetime of 2 Myr).
- **Class 0 ≈ 30% of protostars, i.e. ~0.15 Myr**.
- A median protostellar L_bol of ~1 L⊙, **~10× below the classical accretion prediction** (the luminosity problem). This is resolvable by mass-dependent or episodic accretion but not by a constant-rate isothermal sphere.
- An FHSC property table (lifetime **0.5–50 kyr**, L_int **10^-4–10^-1 L⊙**, no emission below ~20–50 µm).
- The judgment that of 9 FHSC candidates it is very unlikely all are real, since **only 0.07–7 are expected in Perseus**.
- **18–38% of pre-Spitzer "starless" cores host low-luminosity sources**.

## Problem

After ten years of Spitzer and Herschel surveys, the chapter reviews what they show about how protostars are found and classified, how long each observational Class lasts, the luminosity distribution and what it says about accretion history, what the earliest stages (FHSC, VeLLO, PBRS) look like, and the evidence for episodic accretion. Disk formation, ice evolution and environment (§6–8) are also covered but are outside the focus of this note.

## Approach

A review. It builds a combined census (c2d + GB, Orion/HOPS, Taurus; Tables 1–3) and a combined BLT (L_bol–T_bol) diagram of 552 protostars (Fig. 3). It compares the observed protostellar luminosity function with model predictions from Offner & McKee (2011), Dunham & Vorobyov (2012) and Myers (2012) (Fig. 5). It tabulates predicted FHSC properties from the literature (Table 4) and summarizes the variability, outflow and CO2-ice evidence for bursts (§5, §7).

## Key result

- **Lifetimes (§2.2, Table 1; §9)**: Class 0+I lifetimes are 0.54 Myr (c2d+GB), 0.42 (L1630), 0.45 (L1641) and 0.42 Myr (Taurus). Flat-SED lifetimes are 0.37–0.47 Myr. The assumptions are that time is the only variable, star formation is continuous, and T_ClassII = 2 Myr. The authors call these lifetimes of observed classes and half-lives, not absolute durations.
- **Class 0 duration (§2.4, Table 3)**: Class 0 fraction by T_bol is 0.32 (c2d+GB; 63 Class 0 / 132 Class I) and 0.30 (HOPS; 93/222). That gives Class 0 lifetimes of 0.16 and 0.15 Myr.
- **Herschel additions (§2.2)**: new Herschel protostars in Orion raise the total by only 5%–8%, so Spitzer surveys missed relatively few protostars.
- **Luminosities (Table 2)**: L_bol ranges 0.01–69 L⊙ in c2d+GB and 0.02–1440 L⊙ in HOPS. Mean (median) is 4.3 (1.3) L⊙ for c2d+GB and 14 (1.2) L⊙ for HOPS. The approximate c2d+GB completeness is 0.05 L⊙, or 0.2 L⊙ including IC5146.
- **Luminosity problem (§3.1)**: observed protostars are generally about 10 times less luminous than expected. With ṁ ≃ 10^-5 M⊙ yr^-1 and r = 3 R⊙, a 0.25 M⊙ protostar would have L_acc ~ 25 L⊙, and that is a lower limit. The proposed solutions are slow accretion, episodic accretion, and accretion shocks that radiate inefficiently (f_acc < 1).
- **Model comparison (§3.4–3.5, §9)**: tapered turbulent-core and competitive-accretion models (Offner & McKee 2011) and GI-driven episodic disk models (Dunham & Vorobyov 2012) can reproduce the broad observed luminosity function, and the constant-rate isothermal sphere cannot. In the disk-fragmentation burst mode, bursts last ≲ 100–200 yr at ṁ ≳ a few × 10^-5 M⊙ yr^-1 and quiescent phases last 10^3–10^4 yr at ṁ ≲ 10^-6 M⊙ yr^-1 (Vorobyov & Basu 2010a).
- **FHSC predicted properties (§4.1, Table 4)**: maximum mass 0.04–0.05 M⊙ (0.01–0.1 with rotation), lifetime 0.5–50 kyr, internal luminosity 10^-4–10^-1 L⊙, radius ~5 AU (≳ 10–20 AU with rotation). FHSC SEDs show emission from 10–30 K dust and no observable emission below ~20–50 µm. FHSC outflows are slow (~5 km s^-1) and wide-angle in Machida et al. (2008), though they can be collimated in Price et al. (2012).
- **FHSC candidates (§4.2.2)**: nine candidates exist, found by faint mid-IR detections below the large Spitzer surveys' limits ([[enoch-2010]]), by outflows from "starless" cores ([[pineda-2011]] and others), or by far-IR warm dust. Six are in Perseus. With 66 Perseus protostars, a 0.5 Myr protostellar duration and a 0.5–50 kyr FHSC lifetime, 0.07–7 first cores are expected in Perseus. Unless the longest lifetimes are right, at least some candidates are very young protostars.
- **Hidden protostars in "starless" cores (§4.2.2)**: combining [[dunham-2008]] and Schnee et al. (2012), approximately 18%–38% of cores that were starless before Spitzer host low-luminosity sources.
- **VeLLOs (§4.2.1)**: 15 were found in c2d. They are heterogeneous: extremely young protostars, protostars in quiescent phases of episodic accretion, and proto-brown dwarfs. Their mid-IR detections mean none can be an FHSC.
- **PBRS (§4.2.3)**: restates [[stutz-2013]]: 18 PBRS, ~5% of Orion protostars, ~25 kyr, 1% (Orion A) against ~17% (Orion B), T_bol ≤ 44 K, envelope masses ~0.2–1.0 M⊙, 8 undetected at 24 µm.
- **Episodic accretion evidence (§5)**: Offner & McKee (2011) estimate ~25% of protostellar mass is accreted in bursts. Photometric comparisons give burst intervals of at least 2000–2500 yr (Scholz 2012, 2MASS vs UKIDSS over 8 yr) and ~10,000 yr (Scholz et al. 2013, Spitzer vs WISE over 5 yr). Kóspál et al. (2012) find 79% of 51 young stars vary by ≥ 0.1 mag. Billot et al. (2012) find 40% of 17 Orion protostars vary by > 10% at 70–160 µm over 10–50 d. Pure CO2 ice in low-luminosity protostars (Kim et al. 2012) indicates past heating episodes.

## Assumptions

- Lifetimes assume steady-state star formation over at least the Class II lifetime, with T_ClassII = 2 Myr. Every duration scales linearly with this value.
- Classification follows Greene et al. (1994) spectral-index classes and Chen et al. (1995) T_bol boundaries (70 K for Class 0/I). Class does not map one-to-one onto physical Stage.
- Bolometric properties are trapezoidal integrals over the observed SEDs, with no extrapolation and no extinction correction.

## Limitations / scope

- The protostellar duration may be overestimated if up to 50% of Class I objects are reddened Stage II objects, or it may be affected by the unclear nature of Flat-SED sources (§2.4).
- Luminosity-function comparisons depend on completeness near 0.05 L⊙ and on disagreements between Dunham et al. (2013) and Kryukova et al. (2012) (§2.3).
- The chapter says no clear, unambiguous theoretical prediction yet exists to tell an FHSC from a very young protostar (§4.1).
- Variability statistics were still sparse (§5.1), and the review predates NEOWISE, VVV and JCMT Transient results.
- This note covers §1–5 and §9 in detail. §6–8 (disks, ice mineralogy, environment) were skimmed.

## Why this paper matters

This is the reference review for early protostellar evolution in this vault's domain. It supplies the default clocks (Class 0+I ≈ 0.5 Myr, Class 0 ≈ 0.15 Myr, FHSC 0.5–50 kyr, PBRS ~25 kyr), the definition and scale of the luminosity problem, and a careful summary of FHSC, VeLLO and PBRS candidates. All three classes are defined observationally, overlap, and may be heterogeneous in physical stage.

For any search for the youngest objects, two points matter most. First, the predicted FHSC signature is **nothing below ~20–50 µm with L_int possibly down to 10^-4 L⊙**. Second, the numbers work against candidates: 0.07–7 FHSCs expected in all of Perseus means most FHSC-like detections will be young second cores. The chapter's statistical reading of episodic accretion (~25% of mass in bursts, intervals of 10^3–10^4 yr) sets the rate that later outburst-rate measurements test.

## Open questions surfaced

- What distinguishes an FHSC from a very young protostar? Specific theoretical predictions are needed, for example outflow properties, ALMA continuum morphology and chemistry (§4.1, §4.3).
- How extreme are accretion variations, how much mass is accreted in bursts, and how does decade-scale variability connect to variability on ~10^3 yr scales (§9)?
- What are the true durations of physical Stage 0 and Stage I, as opposed to observed Classes? Resolving this needs the nature of Flat-SED sources and the reddened-Class II fraction (§2.4).
- Is the ~17% PBRS fraction in Orion B a recent burst or an environmental effect (§4.2.3)?
- Are HOPS protostars more luminous than c2d+GB ones because of environment or because of incompleteness (§2.3)?
