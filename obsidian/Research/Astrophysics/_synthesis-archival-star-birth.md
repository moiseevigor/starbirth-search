# Can archival data catch the birth of a protostar?

> **Synthesis note.** Cross-paper view of 36 paper notes. Per-paper numbers (verbatim, with sections) live in the paper notes; this note states what they jointly imply. Project framing is included because the invoker supplied a brief (the starbirth-search repo, `docs/FEASIBILITY.md`).

## The question

What does the literature establish about (i) when and how a protostar is born and what the birth looks like observationally, (ii) how often a starless core/clump turns protostellar, and (iii) what else produces a "new source appears" signal in archival infrared time series?

## Sources reviewed

**Birth physics**
1. [[larson-1969]] — defines first/second core; second core born at 1.5e-3 M_sun, 1.3 R_sun; luminosity rises "from a very small value" to ~10 L_sun only once the surrounding gas thins; stays dust-obscured to ~1e6 yr.
2. [[masunaga-inutsuka-2000]] — RHD with SEDs: second collapse lasts ~0.1 yr; L peaks ~25 L_sun at ~2e4 yr; T_bol jumps 10 -> ~40 K; near-IR visibility only at 2e4-1.4e5 yr.
3. [[tomida-2013]] — RMHD: first core lives 650-950 yr; protostar gains ~2e-2 M_sun in 0.7 yr but stays "deeply obscured"; ~5 yr before feedback escapes.
4. [[bhandare-2020]] — second cores across 1-100 M_sun clouds; 188 yr after birth a 1 M_sun cloud gives L_acc 1.65 L_sun; second cores "extremely difficult to detect".
5. [[young-2023]] — review: "no immediate change in the SED" at second-core formation; FHSC 0.003-0.1 L_sun, 160 um flux rises after FHSC formation; 8/13 FHSC candidates survive; <= 1-2 FHSCs expected in all nearby regions.

**Youngest objects**
6. [[pineda-2011]] (L1451-mm) and 7. [[enoch-2010]] (Per-Bolo 58) — best FHSC candidates: L <~ 0.01-0.05 L_sun, undetected (or ~1 mJy) at 24 um; slow compact outflows.
8. [[stutz-2013]] — PACS Bright Red Sources: 18 reddest Orion protostars, ~25 kyr phase; many undetected at 24 um.
9. [[dunham-2008]] — L_int-F70 relation; 15-25% of "starless" cores hide protostars.
10. [[dunham-2014]] — PPVI review: Class 0+I 0.42-0.54 Myr, Class 0 ~0.15 Myr; luminosity problem; FHSC predictions (no emission below ~20-50 um).

**Lifetimes**
11. [[enoch-2008]], 12. [[evans-2009]], 13. [[konyves-2015]], 14. [[kristensen-dunham-2018]] — low-mass prestellar 0.45-1.2 Myr; Class 0 0.1-0.16 Myr (steady state) or 47 kyr half-life (sequential decay); all clocked to a 2 Myr Class II.
15. [[svoboda-2016]], 16. [[battersby-2017]] — massive starless clumps: 0.37 Myr (1e3 M_sun / M); starless 60-70% of a dense region's life, 0.2-1.7 Myr.

**Hi-GAL parent sample**
17. [[molinari-2016]], 18. [[elia-2017]], 19. [[elia-2021]], 20. [[elia-2022]] — tiles observed 2009-09-03 to 2011-11-05; 150,223 clumps; prestellar = no 70 um counterpart (limit ~0.2 Jy -> "candidate starless"); ~60/40 prestellar/protostellar time split; Galactic SFR 2.0 +/- 0.7 M_sun/yr.

**Variability / outbursts (the false positives)**
21. [[safron-2015]] (HOPS 383: x35 at 24 um, 2 mag at 4.5 um), 22. [[fischer-2019]] (1 burst / 1040 yr, 90% CI 690-40,300), 23. [[zakri-2022]] (Class 0: 438 yr, 95% CI 161-1884; bursts > 9 yr), 24. [[park-2021]] (NEOWISE: 55% of protostars variable; 683 yr), 25. [[contreras-pena-2019]] (Class II 112 kyr), 26. [[contreras-pena-2024]] (Class I FUor 1.75 kyr; 2-3% eruptive), 27. [[lucas-2024]] (VVV >= 4 mag: 32 eruptive YSOs; rise 600-1000 d), 28. [[lee-2021]] (JCMT: 0/190 starless peaks variable), 29. [[lu-2024]] (NEOWISE x ATLASGAL: 18% of quiescent clumps "variable", unvetted).

**Data & methods**
30. [[mainzer-2014]] (NEOWISE-R: ~12 exposures over ~1.5 d every ~6 months; 0.67" astrometry), 31. [[meisner-2023]] (unTimely: +0.7/+1.1 mag deeper per epoch; nebulosity-driven fake variability), 32. [[kuhn-2021]] (SPICY: requires 3.6+4.5 um -> excludes Class 0-like by construction), 33. [[furlan-2016]] (HOPS: Class 0 median [4.5] 12.16 at 420 pc; 9/19 PBRS measured at 4.5 um), 34. [[robitaille-2017]] (SED grids; static models can't fit variable YSOs), 35. [[licquia-newman-2015]] (SFR 1.65 +/- 0.19), 36. [[chomiuk-povich-2011]] (SFR 1.9 +/- 0.4).

## Convergence

1. **The birth itself is dark.** Every collapse calculation places the second collapse inside an optically thick first-core remnant and envelope: [[larson-1969]], [[masunaga-inutsuka-2000]], [[tomida-2013]], [[bhandare-2020]], and the review [[young-2023]] ("no immediate change in the SED"). The emergent luminosity grows on ~1e2-1e4 yr timescales, and near/mid-IR emission escapes only after ~2e4-1e5 yr ([[masunaga-inutsuka-2000]]), consistent with Class 0 SEDs peaking in the far-IR ([[dunham-2014]], [[furlan-2016]], [[stutz-2013]]).
2. **Starless phases last ~1e5-1e6 yr; transitions are statistically guaranteed but rare per object.** Low-mass: 0.45-1.2 Myr ([[enoch-2008]], [[evans-2009]], [[konyves-2015]]); massive clumps: 0.2-1.7 Myr with a strong mass dependence ([[svoboda-2016]], [[battersby-2017]]); Hi-GAL: ~60/40 prestellar/protostellar split ([[elia-2021]]).
3. **"Starless" is a sensitivity statement.** 15-25% (up to 18-38%) of Spitzer-era starless cores host faint protostars ([[dunham-2008]], [[dunham-2014]]); Hi-GAL's ~0.2 Jy 70 um limit makes its class "candidate starless" ([[elia-2017]], [[elia-2021]]).
4. **Accretion bursts are common enough to dominate any turn-on search.** Protostellar burst intervals of ~0.4-2 kyr ([[zakri-2022]], [[fischer-2019]], [[park-2021]], [[contreras-pena-2024]]), durations > 9 yr and >= 2 mag at 3.6/4.5 um ([[zakri-2022]]), 4+ mag near-IR eruptions from Class I/flat sources ([[lucas-2024]]). A long burst of an unseen protostar is observationally a persistent mid-IR turn-on.
5. **Nobody has caught a starless->protostellar transition.** The closest events (HOPS 383, [[safron-2015]]) were outbursts of existing protostars; the only systematic time-domain test of starless objects found none variable ([[lee-2021]]); no FHSC candidate has been reported to change state ([[young-2023]]).

## Disagreement

- **Class 0 lifetime / burst excess.** Steady-state Class 0 0.10-0.16 Myr ([[evans-2009]], [[dunham-2014]]) vs 47 kyr half-life ([[kristensen-dunham-2018]]). Whether Class 0 sources burst more often than Class I is suggested ([[zakri-2022]]) but not significant (p = 6-15%), and [[lee-2021]] finds equal sub-mm variable fractions.
- **Variability of quiescent clumps.** [[lu-2024]] reports 18% of ATLASGAL quiescent clumps as NEOWISE-variable; [[lee-2021]] finds 0/190 starless sub-mm peaks variable. The [[lu-2024]] number is unvetted (10" matching, field stars) — the two are reconcilable if [[lu-2024]]'s variables are unrelated mid-IR sources.
- **Post-birth luminosity timescale.** [[larson-1969]] describes a rapid rise once the envelope thins; [[masunaga-inutsuka-2000]] puts the peak at ~2e4 yr and shows the rise depends on initial conditions (no peak for hydrostatic initial clouds). No paper gives the observable light curve over the first 10-1e3 yr.
- **Internal inconsistencies flagged in paper notes:** [[elia-2017]] prestellar/unbound counts (Summary vs Table 1); [[bhandare-2020]] Table 2 12 M_sun core-mass row; [[contreras-pena-2024]] exposure ~1e6 yr (abstract) vs 782,959 yr (body).

## What's settled

- The second collapse is not directly observable at birth in any band; the earliest observable signatures are far-IR/sub-mm (FHSC 160 um rise; T_bol 10 -> 40 K) — [[masunaga-inutsuka-2000]], [[young-2023]], [[dunham-2014]].
- Accretion outbursts of existing protostars occur every ~0.4-2 kyr per protostar and last years to decades — [[zakri-2022]], [[fischer-2019]], [[park-2021]], [[contreras-pena-2024]].
- A 70 um non-detection at kpc distances allows protostars of up to ~0.4 / 1.4 / 6 L_sun at 1 / 2 / 4.3 kpc (**derived**, not published: [[dunham-2008]] L_int-F70 relation applied to the median Hi-GAL prestellar 5-sigma limit 0.138 Jy of [[elia-2021]]).
- The Galactic SFR is ~1.65-2.0 M_sun/yr ([[licquia-newman-2015]], [[chomiuk-povich-2011]], [[elia-2022]]).

## What's contested

- Absolute starless and Class 0 lifetimes (factor 2-3; reference-clock and steady-state assumptions — [[statistical-lifetime]]).
- Whether Class 0 bursts are more frequent than Class I.
- Whether quiescent massive clumps show intrinsic mid-IR variability ([[lu-2024]] vs [[lee-2021]]).

## What's open

1. **The first 10-1e3 yr light curve after second-core formation** at 70-850 um and at 3-5 um through outflow cavities — no model in the vault provides it. This is the single number that decides whether any archival or monitoring experiment can catch a birth.
2. **An empirical W1/W2 "appearance" rate for starless clumps**, vetted against pre-existing Spitzer/AllWISE sources — [[lu-2024]] measured variability, not appearance.
3. **Far-IR/sub-mm two-epoch tests** of prestellar samples (Herschel 2010 vs later JCMT/ALMA), the band where [[young-2023]] predicts change.

## Methodology notes

- Burst-rate estimators are Poisson with k ~ 2-40 events; confidence intervals span factors of 3-40 ([[outburst-recurrence-time]]).
- WISE's ~6" PSF blends neighbours: [[zakri-2022]] shows two Class 0 bursts missed by WISE-based searches ([[confusion-limit]]); [[meisner-2023]] shows epoch-dependent deblending in nebulosity fakes variability.
- YSO catalogues used as vetoes are incomplete for the youngest objects: SPICY requires 3.6+4.5 um detection ([[kuhn-2021]]).

## Recommendation

For the starbirth-search project: a W1/W2 archival search **cannot test the birth hypothesis on a ~10 yr baseline** — per convergence point 1 a newborn stays dark at 3-5 um for ~1e4 yr. It is, however, a well-posed **burst search in "starless" clumps**, which measures the F1/F2 background (hidden protostars x burst rate) directly. The project's own E1 run (repo `docs/FEASIBILITY.md`) is consistent with this: every W2 turn-on found in 19,219 prestellar clumps had a pre-existing Spitzer/AllWISE YSO. To test birth itself, move to the far-IR/sub-mm (open question 3) or to theoretical light curves (open question 1).
